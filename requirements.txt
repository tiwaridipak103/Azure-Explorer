Great question — this is a very real and widely used relationship in institutional real-estate modeling, especially with Moody’s data. I’ll explain how single_family_mortgage_originations works economically and how it transmits into vacancy, step by step, in a way you can also defend to clients.

1. What does single_family_mortgage_originations really measure?
In Moody’s, single_family_mortgage_originations captures:

Dollar value or count of new mortgages originated for owner-occupied single-family homes

Reflects household credit availability, buyer confidence, and housing market liquidity

Highly sensitive to:

Interest rates

Lending standards

Household income & employment

Home price expectations

Think of it as a flow variable showing how many households are able and willing to move into ownership.

2. Core economic channels to vacancy (the intuition)
Vacancy responds through three major channels, and the sign depends on property type.

Channel A: Rent → Own Substitution (Most important)
When mortgage originations rise:

More renters exit rental housing to buy homes

Net effect:

↑ Homeownership

↓ Rental demand

👉 This leads to:

Property Type	Expected Vacancy Impact
Apartments	Vacancy ↑ (lagged)
Single-family rentals	Vacancy ↑
Offices / Retail	Indirect, weaker
This is the classic trade-off every housing economist models.

Channel B: Household Formation (Counter-force)
But mortgage originations also increase when:

Job growth is strong

Household formation accelerates

In-migration increases

This can partially offset vacancy increases because:

New households are created

Not all buyers come from the local rental stock

👉 Net effect depends on local supply constraints.

Channel C: Wealth & Confidence Spillovers
High mortgage origination periods often coincide with:

Rising home prices

Positive consumer sentiment

Local construction activity

This can:

Boost retail demand

Stabilize office demand

Improve mixed-use occupancy

But these are secondary and lagged effects.

3. Why vacancy reacts with a lag (critical for quarterly data)
Mortgage originations are leading indicators.

Typical lags:

Apartments: 2–6 quarters

Single-family rentals: 1–4 quarters

Office/Retail: 4–8 quarters (weak)

Why?

Lease expiration cycles

Moving friction

Construction delivery delays

👉 If you use this variable contemporaneously, the signal will look weak or noisy.

4. Expected sign by market regime
This is where model interpretation matters.

Normal / Expansionary Market
Mortgage originations ↑

Apartment vacancy ↑ (lagged)

Homeownership rate ↑

Tight Supply / High-price Market (NY, SF)
Originations ↑

Minimal vacancy increase (supply constrained)

Rents may still rise

Credit Shock / Rate Hike Period
Originations ↓ sharply

Renters stay renters

Apartment vacancy ↓ or stabilizes

5. Why this variable is valuable in a global model (your setup)
Given your multi-market global model:

Mortgage origination captures macro credit cycles

Market fixed effects capture local supply elasticity

Interaction with:

Home price index

Mortgage rate

Income growth
explains cross-market heterogeneity

👉 This is exactly the kind of variable clients accept as:

“Macro driver with local expression”

6. Common modeling mistakes to avoid (very important)
❌ Treating it as a demand-only variable
❌ Using it without lag structure
❌ Not normalizing by population or housing stock
❌ Interpreting sign without considering interest rates

7. Simple causal narrative you can say to clients
“Single-family mortgage originations reflect how many households are transitioning into ownership. When originations rise, rental demand softens as renters exit to buy homes, which typically increases apartment vacancy with a lag. The magnitude depends on local supply constraints and affordability conditions.”

This is industry-standard language.
