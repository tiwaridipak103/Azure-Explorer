2. Why would it affect vacancy?
Vacancy reacts through several economic transmission channels.

Channel A: Cost of capital → Development activity
This is usually the strongest.

If 10Y Treasury rises:
Financing new construction becomes expensive.

Developers delay projects.

Less future supply enters market.

Lower supply growth often reduces vacancy.

Example
Suppose an office developer finances a $100M project.

At 4% debt:

Annual interest = $4M

At 7% debt:

Annual interest = $7M

That extra $3M may kill feasibility.

Result:

Fewer projects

Less new inventory

Vacancy pressure decreases (after lag)

So:

Higher 10Y Treasury → Lower future vacancy

with lag.

Channel B: Economic slowdown expectations
Sometimes rising yields indicate stronger growth expectations.

Strong growth:

More hiring

More office absorption

More retail demand

More industrial leasing

This reduces vacancy.

So in this case:

Higher 10Y Treasury → Lower vacancy

But if yields rise because inflation fears trigger aggressive tightening:

Borrowing slows

Economic activity weakens

Demand weakens

Vacancy rises.

Then:

Higher 10Y Treasury → Higher vacancy

This is why the relationship is often regime dependent.
