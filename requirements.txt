The 2-year Treasury rate is one of the most informative macro variables for real estate vacancy modeling because it reflects short-to-medium term interest rate expectations, monetary policy stance, and financing conditions.

Since your response variable is vacancy, the relationship is usually indirect and works through several transmission channels.

Let’s break it down the way a real estate quant would.

1. What exactly is the 2-year Treasury rate?
The 2-Year U.S. Treasury Rate is the yield investors demand for lending to the U.S. government for 2 years.

It is heavily influenced by:

Expected future Fed policy

Inflation expectations

Economic growth expectations

Risk sentiment

It often acts as the market's estimate of where short-term rates are headed.

Think of it as:

"What the bond market believes monetary policy will look like over the next 24 months."

2. Why does it matter for real estate vacancy?
Vacancy doesn’t respond to rates directly.

It responds to what rates do to:

borrowing costs

development activity

tenant expansion

refinancing pressure

cap rate repricing

economic demand
