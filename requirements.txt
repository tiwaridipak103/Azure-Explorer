Commercial_Real_Estate_Price_Index (CREPI) and vacancy usually have a strong but cyclical relationship in commercial real estate.
The relationship is not always immediate — it often works with lags, regime changes, and property-type differences.

Here’s how it typically works in real estate economics.

1. What CRE Price Index Represents
Commercial_Real_Estate_Price_Index measures how commercial property values are changing over time.

Higher CREPI means:

Buildings are becoming more expensive

Investors are willing to pay more

Capital markets are optimistic

Demand for real estate assets is strong

Lower CREPI means:

Property valuations are falling

Investor sentiment is weak

Financing conditions may be tight

Market stress may exist
