What food_services_retail_monthly likely represents
This feature usually tracks monthly spending / sales generated by food services and drinking places, such as:

Restaurants

Cafes

Bars

Quick-service chains

Catering / food establishments

It is often derived from government retail sales data (like food service receipts).

Vacancy in commercial real estate is fundamentally driven by:

Space Demand

Food-service sales influence the demand side, because they reflect:

Higher food-service spending means:

More discretionary income

Higher foot traffic

Stronger local consumption

That usually signals a healthy local economy.

Impact on vacancy:

Businesses expand

Tenants renew

New leases increase

→ Vacancy tends to fall

A strong restaurant ecosystem increases traffic for nearby:

Apparel stores

Entertainment venues

Pharmacies

Service retailers

If restaurant spending rises:

Retail landlords see healthier tenant performance

That lowers default / move-out risk.

Office worker presence

Commuting intensity

Business district activity

Lunch traffic rises

Coffee sales rise

This often correlates with:

Lower office vacancy

Population inflow

Lifestyle attractiveness

These increase residential demand.

Higher food-service retail activity → Lower apartment vacancy

Why quarterly lag matters in your dataset
Since your response is quarterly, monthly food-service data should usually be transformed into quarterly features.

Raw contemporaneous monthly value is rarely enough.

The mechanism is delayed:

Quarter t
Restaurant sales improve

Quarter t+1
Tenant revenues improve

Quarter t+2
Lease renewals / expansions happen

Quarter t+3
Vacancy reflects occupancy change

So strongest predictive power often comes from:

1-quarter lag

2-quarter lag

rolling averages

Temporal mismatch
Sales react faster than lease occupancy.

Need lagged features.
