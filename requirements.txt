We are working on Moody's dataset with multiple features and responses for each market.It is quarterly data that means, that every quarter, new record come for each market .It is real estate dataset.

In a real-estate macro dataset like Moody’s, Commercial_total_liabilities usually captures the total debt obligations of the commercial sector in a market (business loans, corporate borrowing, mortgage liabilities, credit exposure, etc.).

Your response variable is vacancy, so the question is:

How does rising/falling commercial liabilities affect space demand and vacancy?

The relationship is usually indirect and cyclical, and it depends on why liabilities are changing.

1. What Commercial Total Liabilities Represents
Think of it as the commercial sector’s balance sheet pressure + growth appetite.

Higher liabilities can mean two very different things:

Case A: Healthy Expansion (Vacancy tends to fall)
Businesses borrow because they are:

Expanding operations

Leasing new office / industrial space

Opening stores

Building warehouses

Investing in growth

This creates higher space absorption, so:

Demand for commercial space rises

Occupancy improves

Vacancy falls

Example:

A growing market like Austin in a tech boom:

Firms borrow aggressively

Expand offices

Lease more industrial logistics space

Result:

Commercial_total_liabilities ↑ → Vacancy ↓

Case B: Financial Stress / Overleveraging (Vacancy tends to rise)
Businesses borrow because:

Cash flow is weak

Existing debt is hard to refinance

They are surviving, not growing

Credit conditions tighten

Then:

Firms cut expansion

Layoffs happen

Office downsizing occurs

Retail closures increase

Result:

Commercial_total_liabilities ↑ → Vacancy ↑

This often happens late-cycle or during recession pressure.

2. Why the Relationship Often Has Lag (Very important for quarterly data)
Commercial debt does not impact vacancy instantly.

Typical sequence:

Quarter 1

Borrowing increases

↓

Quarter 2–4

Capex / expansion plans begin

↓

Quarter 4–8

New leasing demand shows up

↓

Vacancy responds

That means your strongest signal is usually not:

current liabilities → current vacancy

but:

lag_2

lag_4

YoY debt growth

rolling trend

Feature examples:

commercial_liabilities_lag_2

commercial_liabilities_lag_4

pct_change_4q

debt_acceleration

These are usually much more predictive.

3. Market Type Changes Interpretation
Office-heavy markets (NYC, SF)
High liabilities often reflect:

refinancing stress

CRE debt rollover risk

Can predict:

higher future vacancy

because firms shrink footprints.

Industrial/logistics markets
High liabilities often fund:

warehouse expansion

distribution buildout

Can predict:

lower vacancy initially

unless oversupply follows later.

Retail-heavy markets
High liabilities may indicate:

store expansion (good)

or

survival borrowing (bad)

Highly regime-dependent.
