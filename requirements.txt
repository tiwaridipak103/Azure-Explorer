Retail_Ecommerce_Sales can have a very strong — but sometimes non-linear — relationship with real estate vacancy, especially for retail, industrial/logistics, and even indirectly for office properties.

The key idea is:

As ecommerce sales increase, the way consumers use physical space changes.

But the impact depends heavily on property type, market maturity, and time horizon.

1. Economic Meaning of Retail_Ecommerce_Sales
This feature usually represents:

Total online retail spending

Ecommerce penetration growth

Digital share of consumer purchases

Examples:

Amazon growth

Online grocery adoption

DTC (direct-to-consumer) brands

Mobile commerce expansion

It acts as a proxy for:

Consumer behavioral change

Retail channel disruption

Logistics demand shift

Supply chain modernization

2. How It Impacts Vacancy by Property Type
A) Retail Vacancy (Most Direct Relationship)
Traditional Relationship
Higher ecommerce sales often lead to:

Lower foot traffic in malls

Store closures

Reduced physical retail demand

Retail downsizing

Result:

Retail vacancy ↑

Example:

Department stores close

Big box retailers shrink footprint

Secondary malls become obsolete

But Prime Retail Can Behave Differently
High-end experiential retail may survive or improve.

Example:

Luxury retail

Food & beverage

Entertainment-driven retail

Mixed-use retail

So relationship can become:

Market Type	Effect
Weak suburban retail	Vacancy rises sharply
Urban experiential retail	Small impact
Grocery-anchored retail	Stable
This is why global models sometimes struggle unless interaction features are added.

3. Industrial Vacancy (Opposite Direction)
This is extremely important in modern CRE models.

As ecommerce sales rise:

Warehousing demand rises

Last-mile logistics expands

Fulfillment centers increase

Distribution networks densify

Result:

Industrial vacancy ↓

This is one of the strongest structural trends in commercial real estate over the last decade.

Example:

More ecommerce → more warehouse absorption

Faster delivery expectations → more logistics nodes

4. Office Vacancy (Indirect Relationship)
Ecommerce growth affects offices indirectly through:

Positive channels
Tech hiring

Digital operations expansion

Ecommerce corporate headquarters

Negative channels
Retail corporate downsizing

Automation

Reduced in-store staffing

Net effect:

Usually weaker and lagged.

5. Why Relationship Changes Over Time
This feature is highly structural.

Early Ecommerce Stage
Small increases may not affect retail vacancy much.

Mid Adoption Stage
Suddenly:

bankruptcies rise

malls fail

retail supply becomes obsolete

Mature Stage
Market stabilizes:

surviving retail adapts

omnichannel retail emerges

This creates:

threshold effects

nonlinearities

regime changes

6. Important Real Estate Mechanism
The critical concept is:

Ecommerce redistributes demand across property types
It does NOT simply destroy demand.

It shifts:

Retail → Industrial

Large stores → Last-mile warehouses

Physical inventory → Fulfillment centers

This is why institutional CRE firms monitor ecommerce penetration very carefully.

7. Typical Lag Structure
The effect is usually NOT immediate.

Retail
Store leases expire slowly

Retailers delay closures

Vacancy reacts with lag

Typical lag:

2–8 quarters

Industrial
Can react faster:

warehouse leasing responds quickly

Typical lag:

1–4 quarters

8. What Sophisticated CRE Models Usually Capture
Top institutional models often avoid using raw ecommerce sales directly.

Instead they model:

A) Ecommerce Penetration Shock
Ecommerce Sales Growth
-
Total Retail Sales Growth
Why?
Because absolute ecommerce growth alone can be misleading during strong economies.

B) Retail Displacement Pressure
Ecommerce Growth / Retail Employment Growth
Measures how fast digital demand replaces physical retail labor.

C) Omnichannel Transition
Markets with:

strong income

dense population

modern logistics

may absorb ecommerce better.

So effect depends on:

market quality

demographics

infrastructure

9. Why Global Models Benefit From This Feature
Since you are working with:

multiple markets

multiple property types

quarterly panel data

this feature becomes valuable because it introduces:

Cross-market structural dynamics
Example:

NYC retail behaves differently from Austin retail

Atlanta industrial may benefit more than San Francisco industrial

A global model can learn:

heterogeneous responses

shared macro behavior

property-type transitions

10. Very Important Modeling Insight
The feature often works better as:

growth rate

acceleration

penetration change

deviation from trend

rather than raw level.

Because vacancy reacts more to:

changes in consumer behavior speed

than to the absolute level itself.

vacancy compresses

This feature commonly creates:

A) Spurious macro correlation
Because ecommerce also rises during:

strong consumer economies

Need controls:

Without regime handling:

model may overestimate long-term effects

Useful approaches:

post-2020 interaction terms

Institutional real estate investors often interpret rising ecommerce sales as:
