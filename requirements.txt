wage_salary_disbursement is actually a very meaningful macro feature for predicting real estate vacancy, because it captures the income-generating strength of a market.

Think of it as:

Total wages and salaries paid to employees in a market during a quarter

It reflects:

Employment health

Payroll growth

Business activity

Consumer purchasing power

Tenant demand strength

In commercial real estate, vacancy is rarely driven by one direct factor. It moves through economic transmission channels.

1. What this feature is economically telling us
If wage_salary_disbursement increases in a market, it usually means:

Case A: More people are employed
More jobs → more workers → stronger space demand

Impact:

Office: firms need more workspace

Retail: more consumer spending

Apartments: more renters can afford leases

Industrial: stronger goods movement

This tends to reduce vacancy

Case B: Existing workers are earning more
This indicates:

Wage inflation

Higher purchasing power

Better economic confidence

Effects:

For apartments:
Higher rent affordability → lower residential vacancy

For retail:
Higher discretionary spending → stronger tenant sales → fewer closures

Case C: Business expansion
Higher payroll often means firms are expanding.

Expansion often precedes:

Leasing additional office space

Warehouse expansion

New retail footprints

Again → lower vacancy

2. Why relationship may differ by property type
This is important for your global model.

Apartments
Usually strongest negative relationship

Why:

Income directly affects rent affordability.

Higher wage disbursement
→ more lease absorption
→ lower vacancy

Office
Moderate relationship

Because payroll growth often means hiring.

But hybrid work can weaken this.

Example:

A tech market may show high wage growth but still high office vacancy because remote work reduces footprint.

So here interaction matters.

Retail
Indirect but strong

Mechanism:

Higher wages
→ higher local consumption
→ stronger tenant sales
→ fewer store closures
→ lower vacancy

Industrial
Weakest direct relationship

Industrial demand is more tied to:

Logistics

Trade

Manufacturing output

E-commerce activity

Wages matter, but less directly.

3. Why it may sometimes increase vacancy
You might observe positive correlation in some markets.

That happens when:

Scenario 1: Overbuilding
Strong wage growth attracts development.

New supply enters faster than absorption.

So:

Higher wages + higher vacancy

temporarily.

Scenario 2: Sector concentration distortion
Example:

A market dominated by high-paying finance jobs.

Payroll rises sharply.

But those workers may not create proportional retail/industrial demand.

Scenario 3: Lagged real estate response
Economic payroll improves first.

Real estate occupancy responds later.

Quarterly data especially shows this.

Current quarter:

Wages up

Next 2–6 quarters:

Vacancy falls

This lag structure is often critical.

4. How this likely behaves mathematically
Usually not linear.

Low wage growth zone
Small changes have little vacancy effect.

Moderate growth zone
Vacancy drops meaningfully.

Very high growth zone
Effect saturates.

Because occupancy cannot exceed physical limits.

This creates:

Diminishing marginal impact

Tree models often capture this well.

For linear models, log transforms help.

5. Best interpretation for your Moody's quarterly panel
Since you have:

Multiple markets

Quarterly observations

Different property types

wage_salary_disbursement acts as a market liquidity and demand proxy

It captures:

"How much economic income is circulating in this market right now?"

Higher circulation generally supports occupancy.
