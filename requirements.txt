Here’s how it works.

1. What is us_dollar_exchange_rate_index?
It measures the strength of the U.S. dollar relative to other major currencies.

Higher value → stronger USD

Lower value → weaker USD

Think of it as a proxy for:

Global capital flows

International investment attractiveness

Export competitiveness

Risk-off / risk-on macro sentiment

Monetary policy divergence

For real estate, it matters because CRE is highly connected to:

Foreign capital

Corporate expansion decisions

Trade activity

Financing conditions

2. How it affects vacancy by transmission channels
Channel A: Foreign Investment into U.S. Real Estate
This is the most direct channel.

Stronger USD
U.S. real estate becomes more expensive for foreign investors

Example:

If an investor from Europe wants to buy a $100M office asset:

Weak USD → cheaper in EUR

Strong USD → more expensive in EUR

Result:

Lower foreign capital inflows

Lower transaction volume

Slower development / repositioning

Effect on vacancy:

Office / Multifamily
Can increase vacancy if reduced capital delays leasing improvements or tenant attraction efforts.

Industrial

Retail

Channel C: Multinational Corporate Footprint
Large corporations adjust space usage based on international earnings.

Strong USD hurts multinational earnings because overseas revenue translates into fewer dollars.

Example:

A company earns €1B abroad.

Corporate reaction:

Hiring slowdown

Sometimes a strong USD reflects global risk aversion.

Financial tightening happens

This creates a macro stress signal.
