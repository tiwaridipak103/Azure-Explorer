We are working on Moody's dataset with multiple features and responses for each market.It is quarterly data that means, that every quarter, new record come for each market .It is real estate dataset.

For a real estate vacancy forecasting problem, median_household_income is actually a very meaningful macro-demand signal — but its effect depends heavily on property type (apartment, retail, office, industrial) and market maturity.

Since your dataset is quarterly and market-level, think of this feature as capturing the economic purchasing power of the local consumer base.

What median household income represents
It measures the midpoint household income in a market.

If a market has:

Higher median income → households have stronger spending/renting power

Lower median income → affordability pressure rises

This influences vacancy through demand absorption, tenant quality, and location desirability.

How it affects vacancy by property type
1. Apartments (Strongest direct relationship)
This is usually the clearest connection.

Higher median income can reduce vacancy
Because:

A. More households can afford rent

Better leasing velocity

Lower move-outs due to financial stress

Example
If Austin median household income rises from $82k → $95k:

More renters qualify

Premium multifamily units absorb faster

Vacancy declines

But sometimes higher income increases vacancy
This happens when rising income pushes households toward homeownership.

Mechanism:

Income ↑
→ mortgage qualification improves
→ renters buy homes
→ apartment vacancy rises

Very common in suburban growth markets.

2. Retail (Very important)
Retail vacancy is strongly tied to household purchasing power.

Higher income means:

More discretionary spending

Stronger retailer sales

Higher tenant survival rates

Lower store closures

Flow:

Median income ↑
→ consumer spending ↑
→ retailer revenue ↑
→ fewer vacancies

This is especially important for:

Lifestyle centers

High-end retail corridors

Less impactful for necessity retail.

3. Office (Indirect relationship)
Median household income influences office vacancy through labor-market quality.

Higher-income markets often signal:

Skilled labor concentration

Attractive business environment

Knowledge-economy expansion

Which can mean:

More firms lease office space
→ lower vacancy

But post-remote-work, this relationship weakened substantially.

4. Industrial (Weakest direct effect)
Industrial vacancy is usually driven more by:

Trade flows

Logistics activity

Port access

Manufacturing demand

Median household income matters only indirectly through local consumption demand.

Dynamic interpretation in quarterly data
Since your data is quarterly, absolute income level matters less than income trend.

The model often learns:

Positive YoY income growth
Suggests improving local fundamentals

This often predicts:

future vacancy compression

because real estate reacts with lag.

Income decline / stagnation
Signals weakening local economic strength

This often predicts:

Slower absorption

Rising delinquencies

Vacancy expansion

Why this feature is powerful in your global model
Since you're combining many markets, median household income helps normalize cross-market differences.

Without it:

A 10% vacancy in:

New York City

Atlanta

looks similar numerically.

But income context differs dramatically.

The feature tells the model:

“Interpret vacancy relative to local affordability and purchasing capacity.”

That helps transfer learning across markets.

Nonlinear behavior (important)
This relationship is rarely linear.

Typical shape:

Low-income markets
Small income gains may sharply reduce vacancy

Mid-income markets
Moderate effect

High-income markets
Can reverse due to:

Saturation

Migration outflows

Homeownership transition

A tree-based model (like XGBoost / LightGBM) captures this naturally.

Interactions you should expect
This feature becomes much stronger when combined with:

median_household_income × rent_growth
Tests affordability stress

High income + moderate rent growth
= healthy

Low income + high rent growth
= vacancy risk

median_household_income × unemployment
Captures resilience

High income + rising unemployment
may still remain stable

Low income + rising unemployment
vacancy jumps faster

median_household_income × house_price_index
Captures rent-vs-buy switching

Very useful for apartments.

What your model is probably learning
If using lagged quarterly inputs, the model likely learns patterns like:

Pattern 1
Income rising for 4 quarters
→ vacancy falls 2–4 quarters later

Pattern 2
Income flat + rent growth accelerating
→ affordability squeeze
→ vacancy rises

Pattern 3
Very high income + mortgage rates falling
→ renter exit to ownership
→ multifamily vacancy rises

To validate its role:

1. SHAP dependence plot
Plot:

median_household_income vs predicted vacancy

Look for thresholds.

2. Segment by property type
Its importance should differ materially.

Expected ranking:

Apartment > Retail > Office > Industrial

3. Add growth-based variants
Often stronger than raw level:

QoQ income growth

YoY income growth

4-quarter rolling average

Income acceleration

For your Moody’s quarterly real estate dataset, median household income is essentially a structural demand-strength proxy.

It answers:

“How financially capable is this market’s tenant/customer base of absorbing space?”

That’s why it often becomes a foundational explanatory variable for vacancy forecasting.
